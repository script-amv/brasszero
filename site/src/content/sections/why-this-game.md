Brass: Birmingham has been listed as the highest-rated board game on BoardGameGeek (Guinness World Records, March 2025). Up to four players build canals, railways, mills and mines across the industrial Midlands, competing for the same coal, iron and markets.

That makes it hard for a machine in ways that landmark game AIs did not have to face. Chess and Go have two players and no secrets. Poker programs mastered hidden cards, but nothing is built and each hand is short. In Brass, four players hold hidden hands, share one economy, borrow against the future, and live with decisions that pay off an era later. A typical game has about 172 decisions with about 72 legal options at each one.
